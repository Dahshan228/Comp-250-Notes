# Note 5: Big Sleep’s Experimental Caveat

**Quotation:** “However, we [the Big Sleep team] want to reiterate that these are highly experimental results. The position of the Big Sleep team is that at present, it’s likely that a target-specific fuzzer [a tool that tests one program with huge numbers of automatically generated inputs] would be at least as effective (at finding vulnerabilities)” (Big Sleep Team 2024, under “Conclusion”).

**Commentary:** This caveat made me notice that the evidence for AI helping defenders is much more cautious than the evidence for AI helping attackers.
Fang’s team reports an 87% success rate and Anthropic says AI did most of a real attack, but the Big Sleep team says a regular fuzzer might have done just as well as their own tool.
I respect the honesty, and our storyboard needs to admit this.
I don’t think it means defensive AI is weak, though, since note 2 showed that finding flaws is just harder than exploiting known ones.
This post is also from late 2024, and in August 2026 AI found the PHP2Shell flaws (Ntsele 2026), so I need newer evidence before judging defense today.

**Subject terms:** vulnerability discovery; fuzzing; offense–defense balance
