# Note 5: Big Sleep’s Experimental Caveat

**Quotation:** “However, we [the Big Sleep team] want to reiterate that these are highly experimental results. The position of the Big Sleep team is that at present, it’s likely that a target-specific fuzzer [a tool that tests one program with huge numbers of automatically generated inputs] would be at least as effective (at finding vulnerabilities)” (Big Sleep Team 2024, under “Conclusion”).

**Commentary:** Compared to the attack-side sources, this caveat made me notice something I didn’t expect.
The evidence that AI helps defenders is a lot more cautious than the evidence that it helps attackers.
Fang’s team reports an 87% success rate and Anthropic says AI did most of a real attack, but the Big Sleep team, writing about their own success, says a regular fuzzer might have done just as well.
I respect them for being honest about it, and it’s something our storyboard needs to acknowledge.
I don’t think it means defensive AI is weak, though.
It lines up with note 2, where finding a flaw turned out to be much harder than exploiting a known one, so defensive results are going to look less impressive.
This post is also from late 2024.
In August 2026, AI found the PHP2Shell flaws that people had missed for over a decade (Ntsele 2026), so discovery has probably improved a lot since then.
I need to find newer evidence on AI finding flaws so I’m not judging defense in 2026 with results from 2024.

**Subject terms:** vulnerability discovery; fuzzing; offense–defense balance
