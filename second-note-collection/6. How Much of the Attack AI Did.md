# Note 6: How Much of the Attack AI Did

**Quotation:** “Overall, the threat actor [a Chinese state-sponsored group, in Anthropic’s assessment] was able to use AI to perform 80-90% of the campaign, with human intervention required only sporadically (perhaps 4-6 critical decision points per hacking campaign). The sheer amount of work performed by the AI would have taken vast amounts of time for a human team. At the peak of its attack, the AI [Anthropic’s Claude Code tool] made thousands of requests, often multiple per second—an attack speed that would have been, for human hackers, simply impossible to match” (Anthropic 2025, under “How the Cyberattack Worked”).

**Commentary:** “Impossible to match” is the line everyone quotes, including our storyboard, but I don’t think it proves as much as it sounds.
Requests per second measures how fast the steps ran, not how fast a target got broken into, and out of about thirty targets the attack only “succeeded in a small number of cases” (Anthropic 2025).
The detail that matters more is the part in parentheses.
If humans only made four to six decisions, they were mostly approving the AI’s work, so one group could attack many targets at once, which is the scaling from note 3.
Defenders can’t scale the same way, since patching still needs a person at every step.
So I want our storyboard to say AI changes the scale of attacks more than their speed.
I also have to remember that Anthropic is reporting on misuse of its own product, so it has reasons both to make the danger sound big and to show that it caught it.

**Subject terms:** AI agents; attack scale; human oversight; patch window
