# Note 6: How Much of the Attack AI Did

**Quotation:** “Overall, the threat actor [a Chinese state-sponsored group, in Anthropic’s assessment] was able to use AI to perform 80-90% of the campaign, with human intervention required only sporadically (perhaps 4-6 critical decision points per hacking campaign). The sheer amount of work performed by the AI would have taken vast amounts of time for a human team. At the peak of its attack, the AI [Anthropic’s Claude Code tool] made thousands of requests, often multiple per second—an attack speed that would have been, for human hackers, simply impossible to match” (Anthropic 2025, under “How the Cyberattack Worked”).

**Commentary:** “Impossible to match” is the part of this report everyone quotes, including our storyboard, but after rereading it I don’t think it proves as much as it sounds like.
Requests per second measures how fast the steps of the attack ran, not how fast a target actually got broken into.
The post doesn’t say how long the whole campaign took, and out of about thirty targets it only “succeeded in a small number of cases” (Anthropic 2025).
The detail I think matters more is the part in parentheses.
If humans only had to make four to six decisions, then they weren’t really doing the hacking anymore, they were mostly approving what the AI did.
That means one group could run the same attack against a lot of targets at once, which is the scaling that note 3 predicted from the cost numbers.
Defenders are in the opposite situation, since every step of patching, like testing the fix and scheduling a restart, still needs a person.
Because of this, I want to change our storyboard to say AI changes the scale of attacks more than the speed of one attack, since scale is what overwhelms thousands of organizations trying to patch at the same time.
I also have to remember that Anthropic is reporting on misuse of its own product, so it has a reason to make the danger sound big and also a reason to show that it caught it.

**Subject terms:** AI agents; attack scale; human oversight; patch window
