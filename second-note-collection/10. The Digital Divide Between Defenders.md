# Note 10: The Digital Divide Between Defenders

**Quotation:** “However, AI will also aid system owners and software developers in securing systems. As there is a remote chance of universal access to AI for cyber security defence by 2027, there will almost certainly be a digital divide between systems keeping pace with AI-enabled threat, and a large proportion that are more vulnerable” (National Cyber Security Centre 2025, under “Assessment”).

**Commentary:** This passage changed how I think about our research question.
We’ve been asking whether AI helps “defenders” or “attackers” more, like each one is a single group.
The NCSC’s answer is that defenders are going to split.
It says there is only a “remote chance,” the lowest level on its scale, that all defenders will have access to AI by 2027.
Attackers don’t have that problem, since the same report expects criminals to sell AI hacking tools “as a service” to less skilled hackers (National Cyber Security Centre 2025).
So I think the real gap might be between attackers and the weakest defenders, like small businesses, local governments, and anyone running an old website they barely keep up with.
PHP shows why this matters, because it runs a lot of the web, including WordPress sites and shared hosting (Ntsele 2026), and those are the places least likely to have AI defense.
This means our claim needs to be more specific.
Saying organizations need to respond within hours applies to everyone, but only the ones on the right side of this divide will actually be able to.
I want our paper to talk about what everyone else can realistically do, like taking sites offline the way governments did during PHP2Shell.

**Subject terms:** digital divide; offense–defense balance; patch window
